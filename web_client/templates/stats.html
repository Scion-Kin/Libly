*The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsène Lupin, Gentleman-Burglar* by Maurice Leblanc is a captivating collection of nine short stories that introduce readers to the charming and cunning French gentleman thief, Arsène Lupin. First published in 1907, these tales were initially serialized in the magazine *Je sais tout* starting in 1905 . [Arsène Lupin, gentleman-cambrioleur](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars%C3%A8ne_Lupin%2C_gentleman-cambrioleur?utm_source=libly.liny.studio) \n Lupin is not your typical criminal; he's a master of disguise, a brilliant strategist, and possesses an unshakable sense of humor. Operating primarily in the opulent settings of French high society, he targets the wealthy and corrupt, often outsmarting law enforcement with flair. His escapades are marked by wit, sophistication, and a moral code that sets him apart from ordinary thieves.\n The stories in this collection showcase Lupin's ingenuity and audacity. In "The Arrest of Arsène Lupin," he is apprehended aboard a transatlantic liner, only to reveal layers of deception that baffle authorities. "Arsène Lupin in Prison" sees him orchestrating a theft from behind bars, demonstrating his unparalleled cunning. Other tales, like "The Queen's Necklace" and "The Black Pearl," blend mystery and adventure, highlighting Lupin's ability to navigate complex social situations and solve intricate puzzles. [The Extraordinary Adventures of Arsène Lupin, Gentleman-Burglar](https://www.loyalbooks.com/book/the-extraordinary-adventures-of-arsene-lupin-gentleman-burglar?utm_source=libly.liny.studio), [Arsène Lupin, Gentleman Burglar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars%C3%A8ne_Lupin%2C_Gentleman_Burglar?utm_source=libly.liny.studio)\n One notable story, "Sherlock Holmes Arrives Too Late," features a character modeled after Arthur Conan Doyle's famous detective. Due to legal issues, the name was altered to "Herlock Sholmes" in later editions . [Arsène Lupin, Gentleman Burglar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars%C3%A8ne_Lupin%2C_Gentleman_Burglar?utm_source=libly.liny.studio), [The Thief Who Stole Sherlock's Watch](https://www.wired.com/2010/01/the-thief-who-stole-sherlocks-watch?utm_source=libly.liny.studio)\n Leblanc's writing combines suspense, humor, and social commentary, making Lupin a beloved figure in detective fiction. His tales have inspired numerous adaptations, including the popular Netflix series *Lupin*, which reimagines the character in a modern context .
